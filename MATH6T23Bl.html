The Ten Uses of Percent

USE 1 • PRICE REDUCTION

DISCOUNT 🏷️

A percent taken OFF the original price — the shopper pays less.

ORIGINAL
₱500
30%OFF
➜
SAVE
₱150
NOW
₱350

30% of ₱500 = 0.30 × 500 = ₱150 off • ₱500 − ₱150 = ₱350

WHOLE = original price% amount = money savedSUBTRACT for sale price

The store removes 30 pesos for every 100 pesos of the tag price. Find that amount first (0.30 × 500 = 150), then subtract it — the sale price is what remains.

USE 2 • MONEY GROWING / SHRINKING IN BANK

INTEREST 🏦

A percent added to savings (or charged on loans) over time.

₱1,000

saved at

5% / year

earns

₱50 / year

5% of ₱1,000 = 0.05 × 1,000 = ₱50 — five pesos per hundred saved

SAVINGS: bank pays YOU the %LOANS: YOU pay the bank the %WHOLE = balance or loan

Interest is the price of money in time. The bank computes it as a percent of your balance or loan each year — money grows in savings and grows against you in loans.

USE 3 • GOVERNMENT FEE ON RECEIPT

TAX 🧾

A percent added to every purchase, collected by the government.

🧾 RECEIPT

Shirt₱400.00
Jeans₱900.00
Socks₱200.00
Subtotal₱1,500.00
VAT (12%)₱180.00
TOTAL₱1,680.00

12% of ₱1,500 = ₱180 VAT — the government's share, added at the register

PH VAT = 12%WHOLE = subtotalADD tax to get total

For every 100 pesos you spend, 12 pesos go to the government as VAT. Compute 0.12 × subtotal, then ADD it — that is why the total is always bigger than the subtotal.

USE 4 • PAY BASED ON SALES MADE

COMMISSION 💼

A percent of total sales given to the worker as pay.

TOTAL SALES
₱50,000
5%COMMISSION
➜
AGENT EARNS
₱2,500

5% of ₱50,000 = 0.05 × 50,000 = ₱2,500 pay — more sales, more earn

WHOLE = total sales made% amount = worker's payagents, brokers, sellers

Here the percent amount IS the salary — nothing is added or subtracted from it. The company keeps the rest of the sales, so the worker's share grows exactly with the sales.

USE 5 • EXTRA RESTAURANT FEE

SERVICE CHARGE 🍽️

A percent added on top of your bill by the restaurant.

BILL
₱1,000
SERVICE CHARGE (10%)
₱100
➜
TOTAL
₱1,100

10% of ₱1,000 = ₱100 — added by the restaurant, not optional like a tip

WHOLE = the bill% amount = extra feeADD to get the total

The house computes 10 pesos for every 100 pesos of your order and adds it automatically for staff service. Unlike a tip, you do not choose it — it is already inside the total.

USE 6 • SURVEY, POLL, OR GROUP RESULTS

STATISTICS 📊

A percent that summarizes what a whole group answered.

"Favorite ice cream?" — 200 students surveyed

Chocolate 75%
Vanilla 15%
Other 10%
150 students30 students20 students

75% of 200 = 0.75 × 200 = 150 students — the surveyed group is the WHOLE

polls & group results% = per 100 people askedalways check sample size!

The percent converts any group size to a common "out of 100" language, so results from different surveys can be compared. Multiply the percent by the group to recover the actual headcount.

USE 7 • LOADING BAR OR BATTERY

PROGRESS 📱

A percent showing how much of a task or charge is done or left.

Phone battery: 75%
Download
88%
Game level
65%

75% = 75 of 100 parts of the full charge still left

0% = empty / not started50% = halfway100% = full / complete

The bar pictures the WHOLE as a full rectangle; the filled part is the percent. Reading it tells you instantly how much is done — and 100% minus the shown percent tells you how much remains.

USE 8 • CHANCE OF SOMETHING HAPPENING

PROBABILITY 🎲

A percent measuring how likely an event is to happen.

🌧️ 30%CHANCE OF RAIN
4 red of 10 = 40% chance of red

30% chance = about 30 rainy days out of every 100 similar days

0% = impossible50% = equally likely100% = certainWHOLE = all outcomes

Probability compares the favorable outcomes to ALL possible outcomes and writes it per hundred. It predicts likelihood over many trials — it never promises one single result.

USE 9 • FOOD PACKAGE LABEL

NUTRITION 🍎

A percent comparing a nutrient in one serving to your whole-day need.

Nutrition Facts

Serving Size1 bar
Calories180
Vitamin C25% DV
Calcium10% DV
Iron8% DV

25% DV = one serving gives 25 of every 100 parts of your daily need

WHOLE = your daily need5% or less = LOW20% or more = HIGH

%DV does NOT measure the food's weight — it measures how much of your day's requirement one serving covers. Eat two servings and you double the percent.

USE 10 • TEST OR EXAM SCORE

GRADE 📚

A percent comparing your score to the total, on a 100-point scale.

SubjectScoreTotal%
Math465092%
Science405080%
English485096%

46 ÷ 50 = 0.92 → × 100 = 92% — every test on the same 100-point scale

WHOLE = total itemsPART = correct answerspart ÷ whole × 100 = grade %

Because tests have different item counts, raw scores cannot be compared directly. Converting to percent rescales every test to 100, so a 46/50 and a 92/100 mean exactly the same performance.

Which Use Is It?

🔍 Quick Check — Which use is it?
• Price reduction?→ Discount🏷️
• Money growing/shrinking in bank?→ Interest🏦
• Government fee on receipt?→ Tax🧾
• Pay based on sales made?→ Commission💼

• Chance of something happening?→ Probability🎲
• Food package label?→ Nutrition🍎

1 • read the situation2 • match the keyword3 • apply the 3-step method

Each use has a signature keyword: off/reduced (discount), bank/per year (interest), VAT/receipt (tax), sales pay (commission), added fee (service charge), survey (statistics), loading/battery (progress), chance/likely (probability), DV/label (nutrition), score/items (grade).

HOW TO SOLVE 🧮

The 3-Step Method

1

FIND THE WHOLE

the number the % is taken of: price, bill, sales, total items…

2

% → DECIMAL

divide the percent by 100: 30% → 0.30

3

MULTIPLY

decimal × whole = the percent amount

percent amount = (% ÷ 100) × whole
discount → SUBTRACTtax / service charge → ADDcommission, interest, probability → keep as the amountpart & whole given → part ÷ whole × 100

Steps 1–3 always give you the percent amount. What you do AFTER depends on the use: discounts remove it, taxes and service charges add it, and commission, interest, and probability answers simply ARE it.

WORKED EXAMPLE A 🏷️ DISCOUNT

Save, Then Subtract

A jacket costs ₱800 with 25% off. Find the discount and the sale price.

1WHOLE = original price₱800
225% ÷ 1000.25
30.25 × 800 = discount₱200
4800 − 200 = sale price₱600
Answer: ₱200 saved; sale price ₱600.

This question asks TWO things — the amount saved (step 3) and the new price (step 4). Never stop at step 3 when the sale price is what is asked.

WORKED EXAMPLE B 🍽️ SERVICE CHARGE

Find, Then Add

A dinner bill is ₱1,200 with a 10% service charge. Find the charge and the total.

1WHOLE = the bill₱1,200
210% ÷ 1000.10
30.10 × 1,200 = charge₱120
41,200 + 120 = total paid₱1,320
Answer: charge ₱120; total ₱1,320.

Added-percent problems (tax, service charge) always END with addition: total = whole + percent amount. Shortcut: multiply the whole by 1.10 to get the total in one step.

WORKED EXAMPLE C 📚 GRADE

Find the Percent: Part ÷ Whole

A test has 40 items. Liza answered 34 correctly. What is her percent grade?

1part = 34 • whole = 4034 / 40
234 ÷ 400.85
30.85 × 10085%
Answer: Liza's grade is 85%.reverse direction of A & B

When the PART and the WHOLE are given and the PERCENT is asked, the method runs backwards: divide first, then multiply by 100. Grades, survey results, and probability all use this direction.
